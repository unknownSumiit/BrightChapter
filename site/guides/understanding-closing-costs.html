Understanding Closing Costs: A Plain-English Guide

By YOUR NAME · Updated November 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Closing costs are the fees and expenses paid to complete a real estate sale, in addition to the price of the home. They surprise many buyers and sellers, so it helps to know what to expect. Amounts vary widely by state, county, and lender.

Common buyer closing costs

  • Lender fees: Loan origination, underwriting, and application fees (if you have a mortgage).
  • Appraisal and inspection fees.
  • Title insurance for the lender, and often an optional owner’s policy.
  • Escrow or settlement fees paid to the title or escrow company.
  • Recording fees charged by the county.
  • Prepaid items: Homeowners insurance, property taxes, and interest that are paid up front.

Buyers who pay cash avoid lender fees, but still pay title, settlement, and recording costs.

Common seller closing costs

  • Real estate commission, as agreed in your listing agreement. Commissions are negotiable.
  • Payoff of your existing mortgage and any liens.
  • Transfer taxes, in states and counties that charge them.
  • Owner’s title insurance, in areas where the seller customarily pays.
  • Prorated property taxes and HOA dues.
  • Any agreed repair credits to the buyer.

Read your Closing Disclosure

If you are getting a mortgage, your lender must give you a Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Compare it with the Loan Estimate you received when you applied. Ask your lender to explain any differences. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau has helpful explanations of each form.

Ways to manage closing costs

  • Compare loan offers and title insurance quotes.
  • Ask whether the seller will contribute toward the buyer’s costs as part of the offer.
  • Plan your closing date carefully, since it affects prepaid interest.

Beware of wire fraud

Scammers sometimes send fake emails with new wiring instructions. Always call your title or escrow company at a phone number you already know to confirm before sending money.
