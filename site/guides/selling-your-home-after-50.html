Selling Your Home After 50: A Step-by-Step Guide

By YOUR NAME · Updated November 24, 2026 · 7 min read

Selling a home you have lived in for many years is about more than a transaction. It is full of memories, decisions, and a fair amount of work. Taking it one step at a time makes the process far less overwhelming. Here is what to expect.

1. Decide what comes next

Before you list, think about where you will live next: a smaller home, a condo, a 55+ community, closer to family, or somewhere warmer. Your next home affects your timing, your budget, and whether you buy before or after you sell.

2. Understand your numbers

  • Ask a local agent for a comparative market analysis (CMA) to estimate what your home could sell for.
  • Find your mortgage payoff amount, if you still have a mortgage.
  • Estimate selling costs, which often include agent commission, closing costs, title fees, transfer taxes (in some states), and any repairs.
  • Talk to a tax professional. Many homeowners can exclude a large part of their profit from federal income tax, but the rules depend on your situation.

3. Choose the right agent

Interview two or three agents. Ask how many homes they have sold in your neighborhood, how they would price and market your home, and whether they have experience helping people who are downsizing. A good agent explains every step and never rushes you.

4. Prepare the home

  • Declutter first. Less furniture makes rooms look larger. See our downsizing checklist.
  • Make small repairs. Fix dripping faucets, sticky doors, and burned-out bulbs.
  • Freshen up. A deep clean and neutral paint can make a big difference.
  • Boost curb appeal. Trim shrubs, plant flowers, and make sure the front door looks welcoming.

5. List and show the home

Your agent will arrange professional photos, write the listing, and schedule showings. Plan to leave during showings so buyers feel comfortable. Keep valuables and medications locked away.

6. Review offers

Price is important, but so are the buyer’s financing, contingencies, and closing date. If you need time to move, ask your agent about a later closing or a short rent-back after closing.

7. Inspections, appraisal, and closing

The buyer will usually have the home inspected and appraised. Your agent will help you respond to repair requests. At closing, you sign the final documents and hand over the keys. Read everything carefully and ask questions. A real estate attorney can review documents if you would like extra peace of mind.

Protect yourself from scams

Real estate wire fraud is common. Always confirm wiring instructions by calling your title company at a phone number you already know, never one from an email. Your agent will never ask you to wire money by email.

This guide is general information, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Consult qualified professionals about your situation.
